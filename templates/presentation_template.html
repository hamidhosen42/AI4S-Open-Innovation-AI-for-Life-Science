The problem2 / 14

Every nuclear read-out on a chip starts with a stain — and every stain has a cost

Fixed stains

End the experiment

DAPI after fixation gives one time-point per chip; a time course needs one chip per point.

Live DNA dyes

Perturb the cells

Repeated excitation is phototoxic; live DNA dyes damage DNA and slow the cell cycle.

Reporter lines

Rarely available

Engineering a nuclear reporter is impractical for primary and iPSC-derived tissue — such as neurons.

In-silico labeling predicts the stain from bright-field. But a point prediction never says when it is wrong.

ChipStain · Hack2PublishCell number · growth · viability · morphology · dose–response
On an organ-on-a-chip, cell counts, growth and viability all start from a nuclear stain. But fixation ends the experiment, live dyes harm the cells, and reporter lines are rare for stem-cell tissue. In-silico labeling predicts the stain — but never says when it is wrong.
Our question3 / 14

Can we predict the nuclear stain and say — per pixel and per frame — how much to trust it? And does that trust signal beat simple alternatives?

Model

Stain + uncertainty

A U-Net that predicts, for every pixel, the nuclear intensity μ and its uncertainty σ — stabilised with β-NLL and test-time augmentation.

Validation

Tested, not assumed

σ is compared with a matched control (same network, L1 loss) and with uncertainty-free proxies, checked for calibration, imaging shift, a biological read-out and neurons.

Tool

Open and reproducible

CPU inference, public data and weights, three training seeds, and one command that regenerates every result.

Architecture · the model5 / 14

One network, two outputs: the stain μ and its uncertainty σ

ChipStain · Hack2Publish≈24 M parameters · PyTorch · segmentation_models_pytorch
The model is a U-Net with a ResNet-34 encoder. Its head predicts two maps: the stain, mu, and its log-variance, the learned uncertainty, trained with the beta-NLL loss. At inference we average eight rotated and flipped views and add their disagreement to the variance.

Robustness and transfer12 / 14

New imaging and new cells: what holds, and what does not

Imaging shift · 3 seeds per model

σ for trust in distribution; an input check for drift

  • Added sensor noise raises σ in every model and seed
  • Blur or a new modality breaks every model; σ warns in only {{n_shift_cs_defocus_seeds_ok}} ChipStain seeds
  • A cheap input check (focus + feature distance) flags {{n_qc_focus_gate_dpc}} % of blurred and phase-contrast images, every seed; cost: {{n_qc_focus_gate_clean}} % false alarms
Neural cultures · iPSC motor neurons

A small paired set adapts the model

  • Zero-shot: Pearson r {{n_nz_cs_r}}, but nuclei undercounted
  • Fine-tuned on one well: r {{n_nf_cs_k1_r}}; on {{n_nf_kmax}} wells: {{n_nf_cs_k20_r}}
  • Zero-shot σ stayed in the normal range — it did not warn
ChipStain · Hack2PublishNeurons: Christiansen et al., Cell 2018, CC BY 4.0 · dish, not chip
Outside the training data: added noise always raises sigma, but blur or a new modality breaks every model and sigma warns only sometimes. A cheap check on the input — image sharpness and distance from the training features — catches every one of those images in every run, at the cost of some false alarms on a new well. On stem-cell-derived neurons, fine-tuning on one well raises the correlation from {{n_nz_cs_r}} to {{n_nf_cs_k1_r}}.
