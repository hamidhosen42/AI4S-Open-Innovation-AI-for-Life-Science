Organ-on-a-chip experiments generate bright-field images continuously, but to count cells or check nuclei you normally need a fluorescent stain. Staining is phototoxic, it ends a live time-lapse, and on a microfluidic chip it costs reagents and handling steps. ChipStain predicts the nuclear stain directly from bright-field — and, unlike previous in-silico labeling models, it also tells you where *not* to trust the prediction." |

We train it with the β-NLL loss, which keeps the variance head from collapsing early in training. At test time we run eight flipped and rotated views: averaging them gives a better prediction, and their disagreement gives an extra epistemic uncertainty term on top of the learned aleatoric one." |
